# 2026-08-10 — Two retractions, a rebuilt eval split, and a better checkpoint

A day of measurement rather than gains. Two results I had reported earlier turned
out to be artifacts of my own bugs, and the day's real finding — that eval-loss
checkpoint selection had been silently costing accuracy — came out of checking a
claim I had been repeating without evidence.

---

## 1. RETRACTED: the resolution effect was a prompt bug

`orena_count` Stage 4 reported that doubling visual tokens lifted base-model
`number` from 0.092 → 0.234 (lapchole) and 0.193 → 0.375 (heico), p=1e-18 and
p=3e-32. Both were wrong.

`eval_res.slurm` never passed `--prompt-style direct`, so the 2x arms ran with
**no system prompt** while every native-resolution reference used the `direct`
prompt — 1667 characters carrying the FO definition (graspers, scissors and
trocars are *not* foreign objects). Two variables moved at once.

The signature was visible in the raw predictions the whole time: 2x `fo_class`
answers were `grasper`, `scissors`, `surgical instrument`, `scalpel` — exactly
the classes that prompt excludes — and base `fo_class` scored precisely 0.000.

With the prompt matched, the effect disappears:

| `number` | native | 2x | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| base 9B, lapchole | 0.086 | 0.090 | 0.72 |
| base 9B, heico | 0.184 | 0.213 | 0.0002 |
| SFT trained+evaluated at 2x, lapchole | 0.350 | 0.372 | 0.23 |
| SFT trained+evaluated at 2x, heico | 0.489 | 0.479 | 0.45 |

The surviving base-heico +0.029 comes with significant *losses* on `fo_class`
(−0.018) and `open_ended` (−0.043), and does not survive fine-tuning. Overall
accuracy is a null in every arm.

**Mechanism of the false positive**: without the FO definition the model counts
instruments too, so mean predicted count rose 0.45 → 1.40 against gt means of
2.4–3.3. That shift alone raises exact match at every gt level — the same
prior-shift mechanism Stage 2b had already identified and warned about. I did not
recognise it because it produced the number I was hoping for.

A full 9B run (`combined-9b-8r-direct-res2x`, ~4.6 h) was launched on the
strength of the retracted result. The training was sound; the reason for
spending it was not.

`eval_res.slurm` now defaults to `--prompt-style direct` and tags output paths
with the style. The confounded outputs are kept under `CONFOUNDED-noprompt_*`.

## 2. RETRACTED: "eval_loss and test accuracy dissociate" (as stated)

I had repeated this several times. Checked across every run we have:

| run | best eval_loss | lapchole | heico |
|---|---|---|---|
| vision LoRA | **0.3179** | **0.5683** | 0.6210 |
| 9B direct | 0.3257 | 0.5580 | 0.6205 |
| linear-attn | 0.3259 | 0.5509 | **0.6239** |
| 27B | 0.3399 | 0.5457 | 0.6160 |

Eval loss ranks the four v1 runs **perfectly** on lapchole and correctly puts
27B last on heico. No dissociation *across* runs.

The claim came from conflating two things: the 27B's *train* loss fell while its
*eval* loss rose (overfitting, correctly diagnosed by eval loss), which I
compressed into "eval loss disagrees with accuracy". Different claims.

## 3. The narrower version IS true — and it cost real accuracy

What had never been tested: does eval loss pick the best checkpoint *within* a
run? Evaluated four late checkpoints of each of two runs on the full test sets.

**9B + vision LoRA — selection fails**

| step | eval_loss | lapchole | heico |
|---|---|---|---|
| 550 ← selected | **0.3179** (best) | 0.5683 | 0.6210 |
| 600 | 0.3392 | 0.5824 | 0.6270 |
| 650 | 0.3445 (worst) | 0.5763 | 0.6160 |
| **700** | 0.3260 | **0.5846** | **0.6327** |

Checkpoint 700 beats the selected one by **+0.024 lapchole (p=0.0067)** and
**+0.014 heico (p=0.0217)**. Ranking by loss gives `[550, 700, 600, 650]`;
ranking by accuracy gives `[700, 600, 650, 550]` — very nearly reversed. Eval
loss picked the *worst* of the four on lapchole.

**27B — selection works**

| step | eval_loss | lapchole | heico |
|---|---|---|---|
| 400 ← selected | **0.3095** (best) | **0.5666** | 0.6134 |
| 350 | 0.3462 | 0.5519 | 0.6162 |

400 is best on lapchole; heico's apparent 350 edge is noise (+0.001, p=0.88)
while lapchole's loss is real (−0.017, p=0.023).

So selection is **unreliable, not uniformly wrong**, and you cannot tell which
case you are in without generating.

**Consequences**
- The best model is better than recorded: vision LoRA is **0.5846 / 0.6327** at
  step 700, not 0.5683 / 0.6210 — ahead of the 27B on both datasets.
- Its optimum is epoch 1.83, not 1.44, and accuracy was *still rising* at the
  last checkpoint: patience-3 cut the run short on a noisy loss.
- The 27B's 1.2-epoch estimate stands (I briefly claimed otherwise, before its
  checkpoint sweep was in).

**Not building an accuracy-stopping callback.** Accuracy needs generation:
~25 min per eval at 1500 rows, so ~9.6 h added to a 3 h run at every-50-steps.
Cutting to n=400 puts the standard error (±0.025) above the effect size (0.024)
— selecting on noise. Post-hoc evaluation of a few late checkpoints is cheaper
and demonstrably works. The change is a policy: drop early stopping from
exploratory runs, keep checkpoints, evaluate the late ones.

## 4. Eval split v2 — better instrument, worse model

v1 stratified on `procedure_type` alone with no row cap. heico videos carry 400
questions to lapchole's ~80, and heico has 20 train videos, so each procedure
contributed one: **2 videos were 52.5% of eval, effective sample size 6.1 of 11**.

v2 (strata = `(dataset, procedure)`, ≥3 videos/stratum, 80-row cap, explicit
qID de-dup) reaches effective **15.0** with the largest video at 6.7%, and the
format mix now tracks test within ~2 pp. It also removed a 2-row train/eval leak.

Then it made the model worse:

| | lapchole train | heico train | heico test |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 5025 rows | 7200 rows | 0.6297 |
| v2 | 5025 rows (identical) | **5600 (−22%)** | 0.6048 (**−0.025, p=0.0001**) |

lapchole training data is byte-identical between splits and lapchole did not
move; heico lost 22% of its data and dropped significantly. `min-videos 3` pulled
four heico videos out of training. I reported the cost as "−13% overall" and
missed that it fell **entirely on heico**.

**The concentration fix was free; the video holdout was not.** The 80-row cap
alone gives effective n = 11.0 at *zero* training cost (verified: identical 81
train videos to v1). Going 11 → 15 cost 22% of heico's data. Use
`--min-eval-videos-per-stratum 1` with the cap.

Two limitations recorded as deliberate in `README_SPLIT.md`:
- `Gallstone` occurs in 3 of 92 videos; eval cannot cover it without starving
  train.
- **heico eval can never proxy heico test**: heico/train is Proctocolectomy +
  Rectal Resection, heico/test is 100% Sigmoid Resection. No split of train
  measures that shift.

## 5. Counting: root cause, and the line of work closed

Diagnostics on the SFT predictions:
- The bias is **compression toward the train prior**, not undercounting: +0.39
  at gt=1, −1.45 at gt=7, with the predicted marginal centred on the train mean
  of 2.71.
- `number` is heterogeneous — class-count questions score 0.61/0.69 (fo_class in
  disguise), sponges/drains 0.92/0.98; the deficit is clip **instance** counting
  at 0.287/0.349.
- Counting and identification errors are **independent per frame** (both-right
  observed = expected under independence on 305 heico frames), which is why
  vision LoRA lifted `fo_class` and did nothing for `number`.

A 10-frame visual audit at 3x zoom **retracted my earlier "annotation ceiling"
claim** — that was generalised from two cherry-picked frames. The clips are
usually visible. What the model confuses is *shape at small scale*: clips are
small/bright/curved, irrigation droplets are small/bright/round, and errors run
both ways (a gt=2 frame with heavy glare drew pred=5).

Since 2x resolution changed nothing, the gap is **instance individuation** —
pooled patch features give a holistic "how much clip-ness" signal, not discrete
objects to enumerate. The mechanism-correct fix is instance-level supervision,
and the data does not support it: `frames_overlay/` is a burnt-in timestamp and
the `.lance` files carry `frame/h/w` only. No masks or boxes anywhere.

Eight interventions have now been null on `number`: LoRA rank, adapter
placement, vision features, model size, enumeration prompting, teacher
distillation (Opus-5 scores 0.089/0.114, and 0.000 at gt=6), output
calibration, and resolution.

## 6. Contamination found in the existing all-data export

`sft_export/combined_all/train.jsonl` is 20000 rows and contains **all 6252 test
rows**. `combined-all-9b-8r-direct-ddp`'s scores (0.7483 / 0.7676) are
memorisation. `--all-data` now asserts test-disjointness and aborts otherwise;
`--include-test` is an explicit opt-in for the final submission, where the
platform scores against its own hidden set.

## 7. Deployment constraint: 48 GB VRAM

| | bf16 | FP8 |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.6-27B (27.4 B) | 51.0 GiB — **does not fit** | 25.5 GiB |
| Qwen3.5-9B (9.4 B) | 17.6 GiB | 8.8 GiB |

27B is the *smallest* Qwen3.6 — the family is only 27B, 27B-FP8, 35B-A3B and
35B-A3B-FP8, with no Int4 release. So "Qwen3.6 under 48 GB" means FP8
specifically. Official FP8 checkpoints exist (`quant_method: fp8`, e4m3, dynamic
activations, 128×128 blocks), so no DIY quantisation is needed.

Quantise **after** training, not before: the memory benefit of training quantised
is unnecessary (bf16 27B already fits 8×H100 at batch 4), FP8 error is small
enough that QLoRA-style compensation matters little, no FP8 runtime is installed,
and quantise-after is reversible.

**Unpriced**: the FP8 penalty. Measurable on the v2-split 27B (locally
measurable at 0.5666/0.6134) without any training.

## 10. Smaller findings

- The image processor's default band (65k–16.7 M px²) **never resizes these
  frames** — they are 415–518 k px². Raising `max_pixels` is a silent no-op;
  only an area *floor* upscales. Cost me one experiment to notice.
- `datasets` caches by *how* you load: `data_dir="data/frame"` and the named
  config `"frame"` are different cache keys, so prefetching the wrong way leaves
  an offline job unable to find data it has already downloaded.
- Exports store **absolute** frame paths, so they cannot be copied between
  clusters — they must be rebuilt, which is what the manifest pins.
- `pre_evaluation SCORE` is not comparable across runs: the original 9B has a
  1-row `temporal_localization` bucket scoring 1.0, averaging 3 buckets against
  everything else's 2. Use `overall MEAN`, or recompute on shared buckets.
